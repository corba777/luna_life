Wandering Holes

Populations of 100 LLM agents ("cells") on a 10 × 10 torus, round by round. Each cell has to infer a hidden transformation of 4-letter strings from its own private examples. Only 25 cells, the witnesses (marked with a dot), have examples that single out the true rule P_e. The other 75 have examples that a shorter rule, the prefix (the first step of P_e), also explains, so on their own most of them settle on the prefix.

Each round a cell sees its private examples, the rule it held last round, and the messages other cells published in the previous round. It then names a rule and either stays silent or publishes. A published message carries only the name of its current rule and an integer signal from 0 to 7, never its examples. The prompt gives the signal no meaning; cells may use it however they like (one gpt-oss-120b population invented a meaning for it; see the 80-rounds tab). On the lattice a cell reads only its 8 neighbours; on the complete graph it reads every published message. Each square is colored by the rule the cell holds after the round; all runs on a tab play in sync.

What was run, and why it differs by model

One run = one independent population of 100 cells, played from round 1. The common design (world 1, lattice vs complete graph, 20 rounds) exists for every model; only it is compared across models. Everything else answers a question asked of one model.
