When Does Local Talk Become Collective Knowledge?

A hundred language-model agents on a 10 × 10 torus each hold one example of a hidden string rule. No example is enough on its own; the examples together are. Agents may pass only rule names to their neighbours. When does the population come to hold a rule that none of its members can find alone, and what decides whether it does?

Artem Zvyagintsev

What None of Them Knew (report) Wandering Holes (runs) Collective Falsification Code and preregistrations

Summary

  • Rule names alone can carry knowledge no member has. In a world where no cell can identify the rule from its own example, gpt-6-luna populations that pass rule names reach 0.47 of cells on the true rule at round 10, against 0.11 with messages withheld; clearly in three of five worlds, not at all in two.
  • The work is done by each cell's own evidence. Cells take a neighbour's rule when it fits their example and almost never when it does not (68 % against 3 %, counted after the data). Remove each cell's example after round 1 and the population ends at 0.06.
  • An informed individual mostly does as well. One agent given a neighbourhood's nine examples and ten rounds ends at 0.75, as good or better in four of five worlds. The population wins where a wrong rule that explains almost everything traps the individual.
  • How a model responds to names decides the topology. Three response functions measured outside the population predict, without fitting, whether a model does better on the lattice or on the complete graph, and the ranking reverses between models. The held-out tests are mixed and are reported as they came out.
  • Populations invent signal conventions nobody asked for, and a cell reads them, but a convention does not change how many cells end on the true rule.

The no-channel arms kept the four seeded cells (0, 5, 50, 55) fixed on the true rule; with the channel off they reach no other cell, and the no-channel rows count the other 96 cells. The locked prediction, a gain of at least 0.30 in four of five worlds, held in three.

2 · How it spreads: own evidence filters what is heard

89 % of switches to the true rule (442 of 496) came after the cell had received it from a neighbour. When a neighbour's rule led the inbox and fit the cell's example, reasoning cells took it 68 % of the time; when it was just as common and contradicted their example, 3 %. Removing each cell's example after round 1 brings the population to 0.06, below the silent 0.11. Witnesses are needed but not sufficient: in world 4 every cell can exclude the obvious one-step rule and the population still fails.

4 · The response to names decides the topology (world 1 · lattice against complete graph · 20 rounds)

Each model is reduced to three functions measured in a battery outside the population: the probability of adopting the true rule, of abandoning it, and of publishing, as a function of what the inbox shows. Their shape differs by model: a threshold responder (gpt-6-luna), graded responders (gpt-oss-20b, gpt-oss-120b, Claude Haiku 5.5 by the locked rule), a stubborn one (Qwen3-4B-Thinking). Fed into a simulator without fitting to any population outcome, they predict which topology a model does better on, and the ranking reverses between models. For gpt-oss-20b the ranking replicated in six new runs per topology and held in world 3; it failed in world 5. For gpt-oss-120b, predicted before any of its runs, the sign held to round 40 without significance, its predicted levels missed in 6 of 8 runs, and all three locked predictions for the continuation to round 80 failed. For Claude Haiku 5.5, a model from another developer, the predicted class (stubborn) failed, and no run landed inside its predicted level band.

5 · Signals nobody asked for

On the complete graph populations often tie one number to the true rule: gpt-oss-20b in 10 of 12 runs, gpt-6-luna in 7 of 22, gpt-oss-120b in 1 of 4, Claude Haiku 5.5 and Qwen3-4B-Thinking in none; no lattice run forms one. Re-asked with the numbers set to 0, gpt-oss-20b cells adopt the true rule less often (0.385 to 0.281). Haiku cells given a mark on true-rule lines moved in none of 1,200 answers. Across runs a convention does not visibly change the final share on the true rule.

What was run, by model

The common design (world 1, lattice against complete graph, 20 rounds) exists for every model; only it is compared across models. The channel-against-silence and informed-individual comparisons exist for gpt-6-luna only; for gpt-oss-20b and gpt-oss-120b no population was run without the channel. Claude Haiku 5.5 has one, world 1, four runs: 0.127 on the true rule over rounds 10–20, against 0.189 on the lattice and 0.245 on the complete graph with the channel. Qwen3-4B-Thinking has one world-1 pair, run before the seeded-cell fault was fixed (see the log), and it is not used.

Limits

  • One task family with the true rule in a closed catalog; five worlds for the channel comparison, three for topology.
  • Two runs per arm in most comparisons; identical runs differ by up to 0.4 at round 10.
  • The channel comparison exists for two models (gpt-6-luna in five worlds, Claude Haiku 5.5 in world 1); the informed individual for gpt-6-luna only. For the other models every population run had the channel, so their topology results say how the channel is used, not whether it helps.
  • Model behaviour is described as outputs under inputs; nothing is claimed about how a model computes them.

Faults found after publication and every failed prediction: see the log.
